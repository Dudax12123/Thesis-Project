What the guide teaches

The guide shows the same passage twice. The bad version is not wrong; it makes the reader work: the purpose comes last, a hypothesis is stated paragraphs away from the equation it conditions, the same method has three names, and the conclusion claims more than the example shows. The revised version fixes the order, not the facts. Its Tabela 1 names eleven aspects, which I read as eleven principles:

  1. Orient the reader first Orientação inicial
    Before the details, say what the subject is and why it is developed. One concrete sentence is enough; a generic introduction is not needed.
  2. State the main proposition early Proposição adiada
    Theorem-then-proof order: give the result or decision, then develop it. Do not make the reader assemble it from scattered steps.
  3. Keep related information close Associação das hipóteses
    An assumption must sit next to the equation or result it conditions. What matters is distance, not sentence length.
  4. One stable name per concept Coerência terminológica
    Never vary the name of a method, quantity or symbol for style: the reader assumes two names mean two things.
  5. Define before use, and say how it is measured Definição dos conceitos
    A term (e.g. “robustness”) needs its definition and its measure before it is used to argue.
  6. Recall details retrieved from far away Recuperação de informação
    Do not write “the choice referred to earlier”: repeat what the choice was. Name the section instead of “above”.
  7. Progress argumentatively Ordem das definições / progressão do argumento
    Purpose → equation → conditions → results → interpretation → consequence. Each passage prepares the next.
  8. Vary rhythm by function, not terminology Ritmo
    Avoid strings of the same passive cadence (“foi efectuada…”). Vary sentences by what they do; never vary the terms.
  9. Match the strength of claims to the evidence Âmbito das conclusões
    No universal claim from two citations; state the conditions under which a result holds.
  10. Close a subject by its role Fecho do assunto
    End a chapter or topic by saying what it is for in the work. Not every paragraph needs a closing sentence.
  11. Be concise without losing precision Concisão sem perda de precisão
    Cut words, not identity: “the Newton–Raphson method”, not the bare name; keep the word that says what kind of thing it is.

Key take-aways for this thesis

  • Order is the main problem, not style. Most cards move a sentence rather than rewrite it: the research question to §1.1, the model used before the model abandoned, the decision before its reasons.
  • Names drift. Kick / pitch-over / pitch program; T / FT; Δt₁+Δt₃ / tf−tcf; ω / χ. Each is a principle-4 fault the guide singles out.
  • Chapters open and close weakly. Chapter 3 has a two-line opening that is not accurate; Chapters 2, 3 and 4 have no closing. Your coordinator asked for codas in October too.
  • Claims are slightly stronger than their sources in about ten places (“every law as published”, “guarantees the global optimum”, PSO “requires fewer evaluations”).
  • Citations: one source is plainly wrong (a relativity book review cited four times for ECI/ECEF frames), the typical-loss figures do not match Edberg, and two bibliography entries are duplicated.
