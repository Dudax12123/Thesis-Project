## 2. The arc-1 targeting problem

### 2.1 What "arc 1" is

Under `pso_coast` the Stage-2 burn is split into **Arc 1** (thrust) → **Arc 2** (ballistic coast) →
**Arc 3** (thrust), see `run_pso_coast_trajectory` in `Tese/src/Simulation/pso_coast_solver.py:697`.
Arc 1's duration is the derived `t_coast_start = coast_start_pct/100 × T_burn_total` — a naming
trap: `t_coast_start` is the length of the burn *before* the coast, not the coast.

Decoded from the archived `decision_vector` (`T_MAX_2` = 338.7 s):

| case | arc 1 burn | arc 2 coast | arc 3 burn |
|---|---|---|---|
| `gt_baseline` | 242.8 s | 422.9 s | 20.3 s |
| `peg_baseline` | 200.9 s | 189.9 s | 65.8 s |

Every closed-loop law steers arc 1 aiming at the **final orbit** with a deadline set to the *total*
burn time (`pso_coast_solver.py:773`), then gets cut off early and re-targets from scratch after the
coast. The gravity turn flies α ≡ 0 and has no target to be wrong about, which is the whole of its
978 kg advantage over PEG.

### 2.2 What arc 1 is implicitly targeting today — measured

Taking each archive's arc-1 cutoff state, converting to inertial with `ra.get_inertial_state_components`
and reading the osculating orbit with `ra.get_orbital_elements`:

| case | arc-1 cutoff | γ (inertial) | osculating apoapsis | coast ends at |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gt_baseline` | 327.7 km, 7134 m/s | 6.65° | **562.8 km** | 499.7 km, γ 0.21° |
| `peg_baseline` | 318.6 km, 5990 m/s | 12.98° | **559.6 km** | 484.2 km, γ 5.37° |
| `pmp_baseline` | 328.3 km, 6793 m/s | 8.37° | **554.4 km** | 496.7 km, γ 1.82° |

A converged passive law, a 978 kg-off closed-loop law and an unconverged optimum all cut off on an
arc whose apoapsis is **55–63 km above the mission altitude**, agreeing within 8 km. The overshoot
compensates for arc 3 being a finite burn (20–66 s) rather than impulsive: the coast must end while
still climbing, which is why γ at coast end is 0.2–5.4° and not 0.

**Ruled out:** the textbook Hohmann shape (low horizontal cutoff, coast to apoapsis, circularise)
needs a coast of 2 716–2 832 s for this geometry — outside even the raised 2 000 s bound, and not
what any solution does. Do not design for it.

### 2.3 The options, as reviewed

- **Way 1 — aim arc 1 at the transfer apoapsis.** *Rejected by the user*, correctly: it relocates
  the defect instead of removing it. The law would still plan a continuous burn to a point the
  swarm never lets it reach.
- **Way 2 — full Keplerian cutoff state.** Compute the arc-1 cutoff by propagating backwards from
  where the coast must end, hand the law that state, let the law's own `t_go` set arc 1's length.
- **Way 3 — waypoint from the PMP reference.** Already implemented for the *segmented* architecture.
- **Way 2b — the hybrid the review points to.** Way 2's mechanism, with the single shape parameter
  read once from a reference instead of searched. Zero new decision variables, and if the number is
  read from `gt_baseline` rather than the PMP, nothing in the PEG comparison depends on the PMP.

### 2.4 Verified facts that make Way 2 cheap

- **§6.3 flies `peg_new`, not classical `peg`** (`run_results_matrix.py:334`). Earlier sessions'
  notes about wiring `r_dot_T` through `converge_peg` are about a law §6.3 never flies.
- `peg_new_major_loop` (`Tese/src/Guidance/peg_guidance_new.py:153`) already takes
  `v_theta_T` **and** `v_r_T`, so a non-zero terminal flight-path angle is natively expressible.
- The dispatcher already passes them: the `peg_new` branch at `pso_coast_solver.py:470` calls it
  with `v_theta_T=seg_v_theta_T, v_r_T=seg_v_r_T`, both resolved from `gs.target`.
- `peg_new` **returns its own `t_go`** as a value at the arc boundary, before `solve_ivp` runs. Arc
  1's length can be set from it with no event function — which sidesteps the "never latch an event
  out of the ODE right-hand side" hazard that made classical PEG non-reproducible under
  `GUIDANCE_TGO_USE_PSO_PLAN`.

**So for §6.3 the guidance-module change is zero lines.** Setting `gs.target` on arc 1 is the whole
of it.

### 2.5 The constraint that decides viability

The arc-1 cutoff state has three components; requiring the transfer arc to reach the target radius
is one equation. **Two free parameters remain, irreducibly.** One is the coast duration, already a
swarm variable. The second is a transfer-arc shape parameter — and Δ, the transfer apoapsis and the
transfer perigee are three names for that same single degree of freedom. Pinning the apoapsis to
500 km costs performance (the solutions all want 55–63 km more); forcing a horizontal cutoff demands
the ruled-out 2 720 s coast. **If the shape parameter is unacceptable as a searched variable, Way 2
in its pure form is not available and Way 2b is the remaining route.**

### 2.6 Way 3, reviewed

`segment_reference.waypoint_at_altitude` interpolates the PMP reference to `(r, v, γ, m, t)` at a
requested **altitude**, and `_Segments.set_deadline` additionally hands the law the reference's own
timing. Arc 1 in the coast architecture ends on a **time** the swarm picks. Those do not map onto
each other, so Way 3 is not a drop-in for `pso_coast` — it is a different architecture, which is
what §6.7's two segmented cases already are.

The circularity is smaller than it looks for *one number* (the arc-1 apoapsis is stable to ±8 km
across three unrelated trajectories) and larger than it looks for the *timing*, which is precisely
what is unconverged in the PMP reference.

## 3. Why the PMP loses to the gravity turn

**It cannot, if converged.** The gravity-turn trajectory is a feasible trajectory of the PMP's own
problem — same vehicle, same Stage 1, same target, same architecture, α ≡ 0 admissible. The optimum
of a minimisation cannot be worse than a feasible point of it. The 172 kg deficit is therefore a
measurement of distance from the PMP optimum, not a physical result.

Supporting evidence, all from archives:

1. The five-seed spread on `pmp_baseline` is 348 kg; the gap to the gravity turn is 172 kg. The
   scatter is twice the effect. `gt_baseline` moved only +40 kg from 100×500 → 250×1000, so it is
   converged and the PMP is not.
2. The objective ranks on the wrong thing — see §4.
3. The costate search has a redundant dimension and a badly scaled one — see §5.

## 4. Review — the transversality residual (the user's "point 2")

### 4.1 What it is

`dev-notes/pmp_duration_conditions.py` derives the three conditions as stationarity of
`J' = λ₀·(D1 + D3) + ν·Ψ(x_f)` in each of the three durations. Those durations are in bijection
with three of the seven decision variables (`delta_tc`, `delta_tr_pct`, `coast_start_pct`).

**The residual is the gradient of the objective with respect to three of its own decision
variables**, added back into that same objective with weight 10 (`indirect_pso_solver.py:777`).
The conditions come from the constrained problem while the swarm minimises a penalised one; they
coincide because the terminal penalties are ℓ1 on absolute values with large weights — an exact
penalty in the sense of `nocedal2006numerical` §17.

### 4.2 Three defects

1. **Redundant.** The terminal penalties (altitude, velocity, FPA) are needed because the final
   state is an outcome of integration with no decision variable of its own. The duration conditions
   are not in that class — the swarm has a variable for each.
2. **A gradient-norm penalty rewards the wrong points.** `|∇J| = 0` at minima, maxima and saddles
   alike, so a particle parked on a duration-saddle collects the same bonus as a true optimum.
3. **Miscalibrated.** Every other weight has a stated calibration (altitude/velocity "1% → 1.0",
   FPA "1 deg → 10.0"). `PENALTY_W_TRANSVERS = 10.0`'s comment describes the *gauge*, not the scale.

### 4.3 The numbers

`1.0` of `J_nd` = `M_PROP_2` = **92 670 kg**. Validated: the `obj_J` spread predicts 348 kg and
284 kg for the two cases against measured propellant spreads of 347.9 kg and 284.1 kg.

| case / seed | propellant | residual / V_c | penalty in kg |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pmp_vacuum` / 4 | 21 911.7 kg | 0.00085 | **789** |
| `pmp_vacuum` / 42 | **22 032.4 kg** | 0.00071 | **663** |
| `pmp_baseline` / 2 | 20 177.6 kg | 0.00067 | 621 |
| `pmp_baseline` / 3 | 20 400.2 kg | 0.00020 | 188 |
| `pmp_vacuum` / 1 | 21 748.3 kg | 0.00016 | 148 |
| `pmp_baseline` / 1 | 20 322.5 kg | 0.00013 | 122 |
| `pmp_vacuum` / 2 | 21 771.7 kg | 0.00007 | 67 |
| `pmp_vacuum` / 3 | 21 853.8 kg | 0.00005 | 49 |
| `pmp_baseline` / 4 | 20 525.5 kg | 0.00005 | 42 |
| `pmp_baseline` / 42 | 20 515.3 kg | 0.00002 | 17 |

A violation of 0.085 % of circular speed is priced at **789 kg**. The whole gap to the gravity turn
is 172 kg. It is also the **only penalty term that is never zero** — altitude, velocity and FPA all
sit at 0.0000–0.0002 at every solution.

**The decisive observation:** `pmp_vacuum` seed 42 is the production answer we report, its residual
is 0.00071, so **the condition is not satisfied there**. The penalty distorted the ranking by 663 kg
and still failed to enforce the condition it exists for. On `pmp_vacuum` it inverts the ranking
outright — J′ picks seed 3, which has 179 kg less propellant than seed 42.

### 4.4 Bibliography

- Penalising *terminal boundary conditions* is the standard hybrid PSO-indirect structure:
  `pontani2010pso`, `pontani2010swarming`, `pontani2013ascent`, `pontani2014indirectswarming`,
  `pontani2015indirectheuristic`, `pallone2016`. **Those terms stay.**
- `H(t_f) = 0` as a necessary condition for free final time is textbook:
  `Liberzon2012CalculusVariationsOptimalControl`, `hull2008initial`, `pontryagin1987mathematical`.
- ℓ1 exact penalties: `nocedal2006numerical` §17.
- Penalising stationarity of free times **while also searching those times**: **nothing in the
  bibliography**. Consistent with handoff-2026-09-19 §3.7, which already records these conditions
  as our own derivation. In the shooting formulations the literature uses, they are equations a
  root-finder zeroes to *determine* the times, not a term in the cost.

⚠ Rests on the formulation distinction plus our own record, **not** on the Pontani papers' text,
which was not available in-session. Verify against `pontani2014indirectswarming` before prose.

### 4.5 Verdict

Removal cannot raise the achievable optimum (both terms vanish there); it should improve what the
swarm *finds*. The visible floor is 179 kg; the within-run effect is larger but **not recoverable
from the archives**, which store only the per-generation gbest value, not the populations.

**Recommendation: demote, don't delete.** Archive the unweighted residual as its own row field,
set `PENALTY_W_TRANSVERS = 0.0`, keep it as a reported diagnostic and optional feasibility filter.

**Risk:** the hinges are bound-aware (`max(0, H_coast_end)` at the coast upper bound,
`max(0, H_burn_end)` for the final burn), and the latter is part of what discourages the zero-length
final burn. Removal may widen the ballistic-arrival exposure of handoff-2026-09-19 flag 3.

## 5. Review — the costate parameterisation (the user's "point 3")

### 5.1 The redundancy, proven

`PSO_LB/UB[0:3]` searches `(λ_r, λ_v, λ_γ)` in the box `[-1,1]³` and `_normalize_costates` divides
by the norm inside the trajectory call. Scaling only the costate triple of the archived
`pmp_baseline` seed-42 vector:

| scale | J′ | m_final |
|---|---|---|
| 0.25 | 0.778830212705 | 24415.345388 |
| 0.50 | 0.778830212705 | 24415.345388 |
| 1.00 | 0.778830212705 | 24415.345388 |
| 2.00 | 0.778830212705 | 24415.345388 |
| 4.00 | 0.778830212705 | 24415.345388 |

Identical to twelve decimals across a sixteen-fold range. **One of seven coordinates has no effect
on the objective.**

Consequences measured:
- **5.20×** over-sampling of cube-diagonal directions relative to face-centre directions.
- **3.03×** spread in angular resolution across a uniform population (|λ| 5th–95th percentile
  0.455–1.380); the ten archived solutions span |λ| 0.710–1.397.
- **2 of 10** runs ended pinned against the box wall in λ_γ (`pmp_baseline` seeds 1 and 4) — a face
  with no physical meaning.

### 5.2 The dominant defect: λ_r is mis-scaled ~500×

Every archived solution, both cases, all five seeds:

| case / seed | λ_r/‖λ‖ | λ_v/‖λ‖ | λ_γ/‖λ‖ |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pmp_baseline` / 42 | **−0.000467** | −0.246682 | −0.969096 |
| `pmp_baseline` / 4 | −0.001208 | −0.641885 | +0.766800 |
| `pmp_baseline` / 1 | −0.001230 | −0.698301 | +0.715804 |
| `pmp_baseline` / 3 | −0.001496 | −0.726814 | −0.686833 |
| `pmp_baseline` / 2 | −0.001825 | −0.761679 | +0.647951 |
| `pmp_vacuum` / 2 | −0.001438 | −0.770644 | +0.637264 |
| `pmp_vacuum` / 3 | −0.001537 | −0.816445 | −0.577421 |
| `pmp_vacuum` / 1 | −0.001799 | −0.898967 | −0.438012 |
| `pmp_vacuum` / 42 | −0.001862 | −0.872899 | +0.487898 |
| `pmp_vacuum` / 4 | **−0.002167** | −0.982222 | +0.187712 |

All ten lie in a slab of half-width **2.17 × 10⁻³** in a coordinate bounded at 1.0 — **0.217 % of
the range; 99.78 % of the bound is never used.** λ_γ is typically **665×** larger than λ_r. This is
physical: the costates inherit the inverse scaling of their states (r ~ 6.9 × 10⁶ m against
γ ~ 0.1 rad), so a common box cannot be right for all three.

Measured on the actual initialisation: of 250 uniform particles, the expected number starting inside
that band is **0.42**, and **the probability that not one of 250 starts there is 65.5 %.** PyGMO's
`max_vel` is a per-coordinate fraction of each dimension's range
(`indirect_pso_solver.py:906–911`), so the velocity clamp is coarse in λ_r by the same factor.

**The two-angle reparameterisation does not fix this.** By Archimedes' theorem a uniform point on
the sphere has each component uniform on [−1, 1], so the probability of landing in the band stays
2.17 × 10⁻³. Angles fix the redundancy, the 5.20× bias and the 3.03× spread — not the scaling.

### 5.3 Two readings, undecided

- **Reading 1:** the optimum genuinely has λ_r ≈ 10⁻³ and the bound is ~500× too wide. Wasteful.
- **Reading 2:** the swarm cannot resolve λ_r at this scaling, so every run collapses to λ_r ≈ 0 by
  default and that coordinate has never been searched. Under this reading the true optimum could
  differ materially — a second independent explanation for the gravity-turn paradox.

The archives do not distinguish them. λ_γ takes **both signs** among near-equal solutions (seeds 42
at −0.969 and 4 at +0.767 are the two best `pmp_baseline` runs), so the costate landscape is
multimodal regardless.

### 5.4 Bibliography

- Normalising ‖λ(0)‖ = 1 to fix the gauge is standard and the code does it correctly: `Lawden`,
  `hull2008initial`, `BettsSurvey1998`, `Conway2012SurveyMethodsContinuousDynamicSystems`,
  `conway2010spacecraft`, plus the PSO-indirect line above.
- **Scaling and conditioning of the unknowns is the standard remedy** and is the best-supported
  part of either review: `BettsSurvey1998` treats scaling at length, `nocedal2006numerical` covers
  problem scaling, `hull2008initial` is specifically about initial costate magnitudes.
- PSO mechanics / per-coordinate velocity clamp: `kennedy1995particle`, `clerc2002particle`,
  `shi1998modified`, `biscani2020pagmo`.
- ⚠ The spherical-angle parameterisation of a normalised costate vector: **could not be attributed**
  to any of our entries in-session. Do not cite it until checked.

### 5.5 Verdict — revised ordering

1. **Rescale λ_r** so its useful range is O(1). Biggest measured defect, best bibliographic support.
2. **Then the two angles** (`u = cos θ ∈ [-1,1]`, `φ ∈ [0, 2π)`; uniform `(u, φ)` gives uniform
   directions), dropping the flat dimension. Wrinkles: φ is periodic and the box is not (seam), and
   the poles are locally degenerate. Both are smaller than the global flat direction they replace.
3. **Test separately** at 100×250 across five seeds before production budget, or the effects are
   unattributable.

## 7. Defects found in passing

1. **Penalty weights are not in the reference cache key nor in `_ARCHIVE_MUST_MATCH`**
   (`Tese/src/Simulation/segment_reference.py:150–191`, `:247–254`), while
   `INDIRECT_PMP_TRANSVERSALITY` (the mode) *is*. A reference cached under weight 10 would be
   silently reused under weight 0, and `cache_from_archive` would accept an archive built under a
   different objective. Same bug shape as the `ISP_1_MODE` gap that file records having fixed once.
   *Upside: experimenting with the weight triggers no rebuild.*
2. **`PSO_LB`/`PSO_UB` ARE in both**, so narrowing the λ_r bound invalidates `pmp_reference.npz`,
   forces a rebuild and makes every existing PMP archive unusable for `cache_from_archive` seeding.
   Rescaling the variable *inside* the solver avoids this, at the cost of the bounds no longer
   meaning what they say.
3. **Latent:** classical `peg` ignores a segment target's speed — the two `converge_peg` call sites
   (`pso_coast_solver.py:466`, `:588`) hardcode `_v_circular_rotating(r_tgt)` while `seg_v_theta_T`
   is computed and used only by `peg_new`. **Not triggered** by any planned run: both `show_seg_*`
   cases schedule only `gravity_turn` (passive) and `peg_new` (final segment, target `None`), and
   the default `GUIDANCE_SEGMENTS` uses `apollo`, which handles the target correctly.
4. **Bibliography gap:** `peg_guidance.py` cites Brand, Gans & Laue (1993), *Powered Explicit
   Guidance Improvements and Comparison with PEG4 on the Space Shuttle*, NASA JSC, in a docstring.
   It is **not in `Thesis_Bibliography_DB.bib`**, and it is the natural citation for PEG-4
   transfer-orbit targeting if the arc-1 work goes ahead.
